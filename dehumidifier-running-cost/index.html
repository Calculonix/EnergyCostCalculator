Humidity changes the load

A dehumidifier's power draw is not its whole-day energy use

The existing 300 W preset is illustrative rather than a measurement of every dehumidifier. Compressor models can cycle as room humidity changes and as the target setting is reached. Runtime, fan speed, room temperature, moisture load and operating mode all affect the average energy used.

For a closer estimate, measure the complete appliance at the setting you use and replace the preset with that measured draw and your own electricity tariff.

Practical guidance

What can change dehumidifier electricity use?

  • Higher humidity and a larger moisture load can increase compressor runtime.
  • A lower target humidity can require longer or more frequent operation.
  • Room temperature affects how compressor models perform.
  • Fan speed, laundry mode and continuous or automatic modes can change the load.
  • A plug-in energy monitor can replace the illustrative preset with a measurement.

Simple maths, made useful

How we calculate dehumidifier running cost

WattCost UK converts the entered power into kilowatts, multiplies it by usage hours and days, and applies the configured electricity price. Annual figures use the site's 52-week model and monthly figures divide the annual estimate by 12.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

How much does a dehumidifier cost to run?

It depends on power draw, humidity, target setting, hours, operating mode and your tariff. Use measured power where possible.

Does a dehumidifier use 300 W continuously?

Not necessarily. The 300 W preset is illustrative, and compressor cycling can make average energy use different from active power multiplied by every hour.

What affects dehumidifier electricity use?

Humidity, room conditions, target setting, fan speed, moisture load, operating mode and compressor cycling all matter.

Are eight hours per day and seven days per week typical?

No. They are explicit illustrative assumptions, not a claim about typical UK household behaviour.
