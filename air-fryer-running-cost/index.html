Power and time

An air fryer's rated power is not its whole-cooking energy

The existing 1,500 W preset is illustrative rather than a measurement of every air fryer. Heating elements can cycle during a programme, and actual energy use varies with temperature, cooking duration, food load, preheating and the model.

For a closer estimate, measure your complete air fryer setup at the setting you use and replace the preset with that measured draw and your own tariff.

What changes the result

Temperature, time, load and cycling matter

  • Higher temperatures and longer programmes can use more energy.
  • A larger or colder food load changes the heating time.
  • Preheating and the selected cooking programme affect total use.
  • Heating elements may cycle on and off as temperature is maintained.
  • A plug-in energy monitor can replace the illustrative preset with a measurement.

Simple maths, made useful

How we calculate air fryer running cost

WattCost UK converts the entered power into kilowatts, multiplies it by usage hours and days, and applies the configured electricity price. Annual figures use the site's 52-week model and monthly figures divide the annual estimate by 12.

Read the electricity tariff methodology and watts versus kWh guide for the assumptions and limitations.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

How much does an air fryer cost to run?

It depends on power, cooking time, temperature, programme, load, cycling and your tariff. Use measured power where possible.

Does an air fryer use 1,500 W continuously?

Not necessarily. The 1,500 W preset is illustrative, and heating elements can cycle during cooking.

Are one hour per day and four days per week typical?

No. They are explicit illustrative assumptions, not a claim about typical UK household behaviour.

How can I get a more representative result?

Measure your air fryer at the setting you use, then enter that value and your own tariff into the calculator.
