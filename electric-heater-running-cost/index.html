Quick answer

How much does an electric heater cost to run?

A 1 kW heater running continuously for one hour uses 1 kWh; a 2 kW heater uses 2 kWh; and a 3 kW heater uses 3 kWh. At the current WattCost UK reference tariff of the current rate, that is an illustrative calculation, not a universal household cost. A thermostat can switch the heating element off after the room reaches temperature, so actual use may be lower than the rated-power calculation.

For a useful answer, enter the heater’s rating and your real usage above. The calculator lets you change the tariff as well as the power and hours.

Heater types

Fan, convector, oil-filled, panel and infrared heaters

Fan heaters move warmed air and often offer several power settings. Convector heaters warm air that rises naturally. Oil-filled radiators heat a fluid reservoir and continue releasing heat after the element cycles, but the oil does not make electricity cheaper for the same delivered heat. Panel heaters are a form of electric resistance heating, while infrared heaters transfer heat by radiation to surfaces and people in their line of sight.

No resistance heater can create more heat from the same electrical input simply because it is oil-filled, panel, fan or infrared. Differences in cost usually come from rated power, runtime, thermostat control, heat distribution, room conditions and the temperature you are trying to maintain.

Watts versus energy

Why a 2,000 W heater may not use 2 kWh every hour

2,000 W is 2 kW of electrical power when the heater is drawing its full rated load. If it did that continuously for one hour, it would use 2 kWh. A thermostat may cycle the element off and back on, so a plug-in meter can show less energy over the hour. The fan, controls and standby mode may also consume small amounts separately.

A manufacturer’s rated power describes the appliance’s electrical demand, not a promise of continuous operation. Use the product manual, label or a plug-in energy monitor when you need model-specific evidence.

Reduce cost

Ways to reduce electric-heater running cost

Use the lowest comfortable set temperature, heat only the room you are using, keep doors closed where appropriate, and use the thermostat or timer instead of leaving the heater on unnecessarily. Draught-proofing and improving insulation can reduce heat loss, but the savings depend on the building and heating routine.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

How much does a 2kW heater cost per hour?

A 2 kW heater running continuously for one hour uses 2 kWh. Multiply 2 kWh by your own tariff. Thermostat cycling can reduce measured use.

How much does it cost to run an electric heater for 8 hours?

A 2 kW heater running continuously for 8 hours uses 16 kWh. The calculator can price that energy using your tariff and actual usage pattern.

Do oil-filled heaters use less electricity?

Not automatically. For the same delivered heat, resistance heaters are governed by electrical input, runtime and control behaviour. Oil-filled designs can distribute and retain heat differently, which can affect comfort and cycling.

Are fan heaters expensive to run?

They can be when used at high power for long periods. Enter the model’s rated power and your actual runtime rather than relying on the heater type alone.

Does a higher-wattage heater always cost more?

It costs more per hour when continuously drawing power at the same tariff, but a lower-wattage heater may run longer. Thermostat cycling and room heat loss affect the total.
