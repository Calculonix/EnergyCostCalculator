Rated power versus actual energy

Oven wattage is not the same as cooking-session consumption

The 3,200 W figure used here is the manufacturer-stated total electrical power for the CATA CAT72PYBK model. It is a source-backed model rating, not a UK average oven wattage and not a measurement of energy used per cooking session.

Oven heating elements do not necessarily stay on for the entire time. CATA explains that the temperature sensor and controls cycle the elements on and off to maintain the set temperature. Temperature, cooking mode, preheating, insulation, door opening and load all affect the result.

Practical guidance

What can change an oven's electricity use?

  • Higher temperatures and longer cooking times can require more energy.
  • Preheating adds energy before the food starts cooking.
  • Fan, grill and conventional modes use different combinations of elements and fans.
  • Opening the door loses heat and can make the oven work harder to recover temperature.
  • Use your oven's measured kWh-per-use figure or a suitable energy monitor for a model-specific estimate.

Simple maths, made useful

How we calculate oven running cost

WattCost UK converts the entered power into kilowatts, multiplies it by active-equivalent hours and usage frequency, and applies the configured electricity price. Annual figures use the site's 52-week model and monthly figures divide the annual estimate by 12.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

How much does an oven cost to run per use?

It depends on active power, cooking time, temperature, mode, preheating and your tariff. A measured kWh-per-use figure is more representative for a specific oven.

Does an oven use 3,200 W continuously?

No. The 3,200 W figure is total electrical power for the named CATA model when the relevant load is active. Thermostat control can cycle the heating elements on and off.

Is 3,200 W a typical UK oven wattage?

No universal average is claimed here. It is the manufacturer-stated total power of one CATA model and is used as an illustrative input.

What assumptions does this example use?

It uses one hour of active-equivalent runtime per use and four uses per week. These are editorial assumptions, not claims about typical household behaviour.
