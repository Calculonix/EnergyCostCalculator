Power and time

A fan's preset wattage is not a universal consumption figure

The existing 50 W preset is illustrative rather than a measurement of every fan. Actual power draw can vary with fan size, speed setting, motor design, oscillation, controls and standby behaviour. A fan running for longer can still use modest energy, but the result depends on the power and time you enter.

For a closer estimate, measure the complete fan at the setting you use and replace the preset with that measured draw and your own electricity tariff.

What changes the result

Speed, room use and standby behaviour matter

  • Use the power draw for the speed or mode you actually select.
  • Include only the hours the fan is running if you are estimating active use.
  • Standby consumption can differ from active running power.
  • Oscillation, timers and smart controls can affect the total.
  • A plug-in energy monitor can replace the illustrative preset with a measurement.

Simple maths, made useful

How we calculate fan running cost

WattCost UK converts the entered power into kilowatts, multiplies it by usage hours and days, and applies the configured electricity price. Annual figures use the site's 52-week model and monthly figures divide the annual estimate by 12.

Read the electricity tariff methodology and watts versus kWh guide for the assumptions and limitations.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

How much does a fan cost to run?

It depends on the fan's power draw, speed, hours, tariff and usage. Use measured power where possible.

Does a fan use 50 W all the time?

Not necessarily. The 50 W preset is illustrative and actual draw can vary by fan and setting.

Are eight hours per day and seven days per week typical?

No. They are explicit illustrative assumptions, not a claim about typical UK household behaviour.

How can I get a more representative result?

Measure the complete fan at the setting you use, then enter that value and your own tariff into the calculator.
