Simple maths, made useful

Why rated wattage is not the same as average usage

A fridge's compressor usually runs in bursts. A 150 W compressor rating does not mean it uses 150 W all the time. If it runs for around 30% of the time, the average draw is roughly 45 W. That average power is the number that matters most for a 24/7 running-cost estimate.

In other words, the nameplate rating tells you the load when the compressor is active, while the duty cycle tells you how often that load is happening. The calculator above uses the same engine as the rest of the site, but this page intentionally starts from the average draw rather than the peak rated draw.

What affects fridge use

Why some fridges use more than others

  • Warmer rooms make the compressor run more often.
  • Frequent door opening and a full fridge increase heat gain.
  • Older or less efficient compressors can have a higher duty cycle.
  • Freezer settings and temperature set points change how hard the compressor works.
  • Rated power only tells you the load when the compressor is active, not the long-term average.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

How much does a fridge cost to run per day?

It depends on the average draw, the compressor duty cycle and the electricity tariff. A 150 W fridge with a 30% duty cycle averages about 45 W, which is much lower than 150 W sustained over the whole day.

Is the rated wattage on the label useless?

No. It is still useful for understanding the compressor's maximum load, but it is not the same as the average power draw over a 24-hour period. A duty cycle estimate is normally more relevant for a fridge.

Why do different fridges cost different amounts?

They vary in size, insulation, compressor efficiency, room temperature and usage pattern. The compressor may need to run more often in summer or when the door is opened frequently.

Should I use 45 W or 150 W in the calculator?

For a fridge, 45 W is a better starting point for a realistic all-day estimate if you are using a 150 W compressor with a 30% duty cycle. You can adjust the power value to match the duty cycle you expect in your home.

What is the best source of truth?

The manufacturer label gives a useful maximum load value, but the more realistic estimate is the average power you expect from the appliance's duty cycle. Use your own tariff and the running pattern you really expect for a closer result.
