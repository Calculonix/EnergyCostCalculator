Why this is different

Why washing machines are not like simple resistive appliances

A washing machine does not usually draw its maximum rated power for the entire programme. The heating element can be the main electricity load, but the motor and pump often use less power than the heating stage. Programme type matters too: a 40°C cycle, a cooler eco cycle and a short quick wash can all produce different energy use even on the same machine.

The rated wattage is still useful because it tells you what sort of power the machine can draw at some point in the cycle. It is not the same thing as the total energy consumed in a full wash. For many washing machines, a manufacturer’s tested kWh-per-cycle figure is more useful than simply multiplying the nameplate wattage by the declared cycle time.

Temperature matters

20°C, 30°C, 40°C and 60°C

Temperature selection matters because heating water generally dominates much of the electricity use. Cooler temperatures usually use less electricity, while hotter settings can require more energy to heat the water and dry the load. The exact saving varies by machine, programme, load size, inlet water temperature and the model’s efficiency.

Energy label guidance

What to look for on a modern washing-machine label

If the machine provides a tested kWh-per-cycle figure for the relevant programme, that figure is usually more useful than guessing from the nameplate wattage. The energy label or product fiche may also show water use, programme duration and efficiency class. In other words, if the manufacturer gives a tested cycle energy figure, prefer that over a rough calculation based only on the maximum power rating.

For a more accurate estimate, enter your own energy tariff into the calculator above instead of assuming a default rate. The quickest route is to use the machine’s own programme energy figure and your actual tariff, then adjust the numbers to reflect your real wash frequency.

Practical advice

Ways to reduce washing-machine electricity use

  • Wash at lower temperatures when the fabric care label allows it.
  • Use eco programmes where they are suitable for the load.
  • Run fuller loads instead of repeating small washes.
  • Use the machine’s published kWh-per-cycle figure when available.
  • Do not assume a higher wattage automatically means a more expensive wash.
  • Use the actual unit rate from your electricity tariff rather than a generic estimate.

Simple maths, made useful

How we calculate washing-machine running cost

WattCost UK converts the machine’s power into kilowatts, multiplies it by the hours used and then applies your electricity price. Annual figures use a 52-week model, and monthly figures divide the annual estimate by 12. The default rate comes from the current central tariff held in the project data file.

Read the electricity tariff methodology for the source, review model and limitations. The watts versus kWh guide explains why rated power and total energy use are not the same thing.

Common questions

Frequently asked questions

How much does a washing machine cost per hour?

At 2,000 W and the current tariff, a full hour would be roughly 53p if the machine drew that full power for the whole hour. Real cycles are often lower than that because the heating element does not run continuously for the entire programme.

How much does a washing machine cost per cycle?

It depends on the programme, load size, temperature and your electricity tariff. Use the calculator above with the machine’s own runtime or the appliance’s published kWh-per-cycle figure for a clearer estimate.

Does a more powerful washing machine use more energy?

It can, but not automatically. Higher maximum power does not mean the machine uses that power for the whole cycle. Programme choice, temperature, efficiency and load all matter.

Are eco programmes cheaper?

They often are, because they heat water less aggressively and can take longer. The exact saving depends on the model, load size and programme design.

What is the best source of truth?

If the manufacturer provides a tested kWh-per-cycle value for the relevant programme, prefer that over a rough wattage-based estimate. Your actual electricity tariff still matters, and the calculator lets you enter that value.
